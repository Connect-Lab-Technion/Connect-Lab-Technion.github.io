## Academic path

Daniel received B.Sc. and M.Eng. degrees in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science from MIT, worked as a research engineer on audio-compression methods at Texas Instruments in Tsukuba, and completed his Ph.D. in Aeronautics and Astronautics at the University of Washington with Mehran Mesbahi. After postdoctoral work with Frank Allgöwer at the University of Stuttgart, he joined the Technion in 2012.

## Research profile

His group studies how the structure of an interconnection graph changes the behavior of a dynamical system. Current themes include directed and symmetry-constrained formation control, graph-based model reduction and network identification, passivity for nonlinear networked systems, resilient coordination, and distributed optimization.

The work is primarily theoretical, with robotic and energy-system experiments used to reveal where sensing, communication, and actuation constraints alter the mathematical picture.
