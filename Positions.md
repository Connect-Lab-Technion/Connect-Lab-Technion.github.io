## Postdoctoral research fellows

Multiple postdoctoral research fellow positions are available in control and coordination of multi-agent systems. Fellows work with Prof. Daniel Zelazo and the Connect Lab while developing an independent research line within the group's broader programme.

The appointment is full-time and initially for one year, with possible extension subject to funding and satisfactory progress. Support for relevant conference travel and research expenses is available, and the starting date is flexible.

</div>
<aside class="positions-track__aside" markdown="1">

### A strong fit

- Ph.D. completed or near completion
- Background in control, applied mathematics, robotics, optimization, or network science
- Ability to formulate and communicate rigorous research questions

### To apply

Send a cover letter, CV, and up to three representative publications to [Daniel Zelazo](mailto:dzelazo@technion.ac.il).

</aside>

## M.Sc. and Ph.D. students

Graduate research in the lab develops new theory and methods for interconnected dynamical systems. Topics evolve with the student and the research question, but current directions include rigidity and formation control, passivity, resilient coordination, network identification, event-triggered systems, and graph-based optimization.

Students from Aerospace Engineering and related Technion programmes are welcome. A strong mathematical foundation is important; prior experience with every topic is not expected.

</div>
<aside class="positions-track__aside" markdown="1">

### Useful preparation

- Linear systems and control
- Linear algebra and optimization
- Graph theory or dynamical systems
- Clear technical writing and computational experimentation

### Start a conversation

Email a CV, transcript, and a short note describing the questions that interest you to [Daniel Zelazo](mailto:dzelazo@technion.ac.il).

</aside>
</section>

## Undergraduate students

Undergraduate projects are a way to enter research early—through a focused theoretical question, a simulation study, or the development of a robotic testbed. Projects may be taken for course credit or developed as hourly research work when funding is available.

Browse the [current student projects](/student_projects/) for examples, but do not feel limited to that list. A good project can also begin with a question you bring to the lab.

</div>
<aside class="positions-track__aside" markdown="1">

### Good ways to begin

- Read one of the lab's recent papers
- Identify a course topic you want to explore more deeply
- Bring a small simulation, proof idea, or platform concept

[Contact the lab](mailto:dzelazo@technion.ac.il) to discuss a possible project.

</aside>
