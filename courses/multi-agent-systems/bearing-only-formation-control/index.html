About the examples and measurements

The first three examples use a true square with vertices (-1,-1), (1,-1), (1,1), (-1,1). Its four perimeter edges and diagonal (1,3) have bearing rank 5, which is the required rank 2n-3. The target centroid is the intersection of its geometric diagonals. The target and initial states share their centroid and RMS scale; no positions are recentered or rescaled during integration. At the exact reflection, the control is zero and the bearing error stays at 2 sqrt(5).

The 100-agent example uses a triangulated 10 by 10 square grid with 261 edges and bearing rank 197. The 144-agent example uses a periodic triangulated torus mesh with 432 edges and bearing rank 428. Both simulate the same common-frame nonlinear controller with gain one. They are kinematic examples, not orbital simulations. Numerical checks include tighter independent integrations, conservation, and sampled inter-agent separations. These examples do not establish general collision avoidance.

The satellite movie preserves the supplied original animation (640 by 476) inside the common lecture layout; enlarging it does not add source detail. It illustrates the paper's controller using local body-frame bearings and relative attitudes. Attitude synchronization generates a common frame, and the desired bearings hold in that synchronized frame. The final global orientation is free. It is an original visualization, not a new orbital simulation or a reconstruction of unavailable trajectory data.

The newly simulated movies track stacked bearing and position error norms, their base-10 logarithms, and numerical drift in centroid and RMS scale. All use fixed world coordinates. The paper reference for attitude consensus and formation control is Zhao and Zelazo, Bearing Rigidity and Almost Global Bearing-Only Formation Stabilization, IEEE TAC 61(5), 2016, Section IV, Eqs. (19)-(21), Theorem 15.
