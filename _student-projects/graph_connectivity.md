Graphs provide the language for describing who senses, communicates with, or influences whom in a multi-agent system. Connectivity is not simply a yes-or-no property: Laplacian eigenvalues quantify how rapidly agreement is reached, how robustly the network withstands failures, and how disturbances propagate.

## The question

How should links be added or removed so that a network remains sparse while meeting a prescribed connectivity or performance target?

## What the project can include

- Compare algebraic-connectivity and effective-resistance metrics on graph families.
- Develop link-addition or link-removal heuristics and test their limits.
- Study how topology design changes consensus speed or disturbance rejection.
- Build numerical examples that make the spectral trade-offs visible.
